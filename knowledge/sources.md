# Sources used by the tip cards

Short keys (the `source:` line of each card) map to these references. The cards are short
paraphrases written for this project, not quotes. Check the reference before citing it in a report.

- **SUSO**: *Stand up, Speak out: The Practice and Ethics of Public Speaking.* University of Minnesota
  Libraries Publishing, 2016. Open textbook, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. https://open.lib.umn.edu/publicspeaking/
  (chapters on public speaking anxiety and on delivery: rate, pauses, vocal variety, eye contact).
- **EPS**: Tucker, B. et al. *Exploring Public Speaking: The Free College Public Speaking Textbook.*
  Galileo Open Learning Materials / University System of Georgia. Open textbook (CC BY / CC BY-SA, see edition).
  (chapters on speaking anxiety and on delivery).
- **Bodie 2010**: Bodie, G. D. (2010). A racing heart, rattling knees, and ruminative thoughts: Defining,
  explaining, and treating public speaking anxiety. *Communication Education*, 59(1), 70-105.
- **Brooks 2014**: Brooks, A. W. (2014). Get excited: Reappraising pre-performance anxiety as excitement.
  *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General*, 143(3), 1144-1158. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035325
- **Zaccaro 2018**: Zaccaro, A. et al. (2018). How breath-control can change your life: A systematic review on
  psycho-physiological correlates of slow breathing. *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*, 12, 353.
  https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2018.00353
- **Balban 2023**: Balban, M. Y. et al. (2023). Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce
  physiological arousal. *Cell Reports Medicine*, 4(1), 100895. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895
- **Kraft 2012**: Kraft, T. L., & Pressman, S. D. (2012). Grin and bear it: The influence of manipulated facial
  expression on the stress response. *Psychological Science*, 23(11), 1372-1378.
- **Clark 2002**: Clark, H. H., & Fox Tree, J. E. (2002). Using uh and um in spontaneous speaking.
  *Cognition*, 84(1), 73-111.
- **Kleinke 1986**: Kleinke, C. L. (1986). Gaze and eye contact: A research review. *Psychological Bulletin*,
  100(1), 78-100. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.100.1.78
- **Niebuhr 2016**: Niebuhr, O., Voße, J., & Brem, A. (2016). What makes a charismatic speaker? A computer-based
  acoustic-prosodic analysis of Steve Jobs tone of voice. *Computers in Human Behavior*, 64, 366-382.
- **Goldin-Meadow 2013**: Goldin-Meadow, S., & Alibali, M. W. (2013). Gesture's role in speaking, learning, and
  creating language. *Annual Review of Psychology*, 64, 257-283. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-113011-143802
- **Krauss 1998**: Krauss, R. M. (1998). Why do we gesture when we speak? *Current Directions in Psychological
  Science*, 7(2), 54-60. https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-8721.ep13175642
- **Kimble 1991**: Kimble, C. E., & Seidel, S. D. (1991). Vocal signs of confidence. *Journal of Nonverbal
  Behavior*, 15(2), 99-105.
- **Beltzer 2014**: Beltzer, M. L., Nock, M. K., Peters, B. J., & Jamieson, J. P. (2014). Rethinking butterflies:
  The affective, physiological, and performance effects of reappraising arousal during social evaluation.
  *Emotion*, 14(4), 761-768. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0036326
- **Beilock 2001**: Beilock, S. L., & Carr, T. H. (2001). On the fragility of skilled performance: What governs
  choking under pressure? *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General*, 130(4), 701-725.
  https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.130.4.701
- **Murdock 1962**: Murdock, B. B. (1962). The serial position effect of free recall. *Journal of Experimental
  Psychology*, 64(5), 482-488. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0045106
