# Tip cards: face+voice and over-time patterns (reading notes, flat delivery, shrinking, fading, sliding)
# Same format as calm.md. Short paraphrases written for this project; full references in ../sources.md.

## notes-glance-up
strategy: glance_speak
patterns: reading_notes, eye_contact_low
hint: Glance, look up, then speak
action: Read three words, look up, say the sentence to the lens
pause: Pause. Find your next point, look up, then say it
why: Speakers who look at their listener are seen as more attentive and credible.
source: Kleinke 1986

A large review of gaze research found that people who look at their listener are judged as more attentive,
competent and credible than people who look away. Reading a script pulls the eyes down and takes that away.
The habit that fixes it is "glance, look up, speak": take in a few words in silence, then deliver them while
looking at the audience (here: the camera), and only look down again at the end of the thought.

## notes-keywords
strategy: glance_speak
patterns: reading_notes
hint: Keywords, not sentences
action: Say this idea in your own words, not the script's
pause: Pause. Look up and explain this point as if to a friend
why: Talking from an outline keeps your eyes up and your voice natural.
source: SUSO

Delivery chapters compare reading a manuscript with speaking extemporaneously from brief notes. Reading tends to
sound flat and breaks eye contact, because the voice follows the written sentence instead of the idea. Notes with
only keywords force you to rebuild each sentence as you speak, which brings back natural pitch, pauses and
eye contact. A practice run is a good moment to stop leaning on the script.

## flat-melody
strategy: voice_vary
patterns: flat_affect, monotone, reading_notes
hint: Let your pitch move
action: Lift your voice on the key word, drop it at the end
pause: Pause. Say your next key sentence as if it is news
why: Pitch range and variation are what make speakers sound charismatic.
source: Niebuhr 2016

An acoustic analysis of a famously charismatic business speaker found that listeners' impression of charisma went
with a wide pitch range, frequent pitch changes, a lively rate and clear emphasis on key words. A flat pitch
sounds bored or unsure even when the content is good. Raising the pitch on the important word and letting it fall
at the end of the sentence is the simplest way to sound engaged.

## flat-gesture
strategy: gesture
patterns: flat_affect, low_energy, monotone
hint: Use your hands
action: Show your next idea with one clear hand gesture
pause: Pause. Free your hands, then say the next point with them
why: Gesturing helps you find words and makes your voice livelier.
source: Goldin-Meadow 2013

Research on gesture shows it is part of speaking, not decoration: speakers gesture even when nobody can see them,
and gesturing helps them organise and express ideas. Gestures and voice move together, so freeing the hands
tends to bring back emphasis and rhythm in the voice. Stiff arms and a flat voice usually come as a pair.

## flat-face-warm
strategy: smile_relax
patterns: flat_affect
hint: Let your face show it
action: Smile at the camera on your next example
pause: Pause. Think of why this point matters, smile, go on
why: Your face tells listeners how to feel about what you say.
source: SUSO

Nonverbal delivery includes facial expression: listeners read the speaker's face to know whether a point is
serious, surprising or good news. A neutral, "blank" face while talking makes even interesting content seem
unimportant. Letting the face follow the meaning (a smile on a good example, raised eyebrows on a question) makes
the delivery feel conversational.

## shrink-volume
strategy: energy_up
patterns: shrinking, confidence_drop, low_energy
hint: Speak to the back wall
action: Say your next sentence a bit louder than feels normal
pause: Pause. Breathe in deep, then restart louder
why: A louder voice is heard as a more confident one.
source: Kimble 1991

In experiments on vocal confidence, people who answered louder and with less hesitation were judged, and felt,
more confident. Nerves often make the voice quieter and the body smaller, which the audience hears as doubt.
Deliberately projecting a little more than feels natural, aiming the voice at the back of the room, counters
that and is easier than trying to "feel" confident first.

## shrink-exhale-speak
strategy: breathing
patterns: shrinking, stress_creep, sustained_stress
hint: Breathe out, then speak up
action: At the full stop, long breath out, then a full breath in
pause: Pause. Breathe out slowly, fill your lungs, then speak
why: A slow exhale calms you; a full breath gives your voice its power back.
source: Zaccaro 2018

Slow breathing with a long exhale is linked to lower arousal. Under stress, breathing also gets shallow, which is
one reason the voice gets quieter and thinner. Letting one slow breath out at a sentence boundary and then taking a
full breath before the next sentence both calms the body and gives the voice enough air to carry.

## reappraise-arousal
strategy: reframe
patterns: shrinking, stress_creep, sustained_stress, confidence_drop
hint: Your body is getting ready
action: Think: "this energy helps me perform"
pause: Pause. Tell yourself the racing heart is fuel, then go on
why: Seeing arousal as helpful improved performance and lowered stress in studies.
source: Beltzer 2014

In a study with a stressful speech task, people told that a racing heart and butterflies help performance showed
less anxious behaviour and performed better than people who were not. The body's arousal is the same; what changes
is reading it as a resource instead of a threat. This works with the arousal instead of fighting it.

## finish-strong
strategy: finish_strong
patterns: low_energy, monotone
hint: Finish this part strong
action: Lift your voice for your next key sentence
pause: Pause. Stand tall, then say your next point like a headline
why: Listeners remember the end best, so fading energy costs most there.
source: Murdock 1962

Memory research shows a recency effect: the last items in a sequence are remembered better than the middle ones.
Delivery textbooks make the same point for speeches: the conclusion is what the audience takes away. Energy
naturally drops as a talk goes on, and it often fades exactly where it matters most. Noticing the fade and lifting
volume and pitch for the closing ideas keeps the end memorable.

## energy-reset
strategy: energy_up
patterns: low_energy, flat_affect
hint: Reset your energy
action: Stand tall, breathe in, restart this point with energy
pause: Pause. Shake out your hands, breathe, then restart the point
why: A short physical reset lifts voice and posture together.
source: EPS

Delivery chapters treat energy as physical: posture, breath and movement feed the voice. A slumped posture limits
breath and volume. A brief reset (standing tall, a full breath, a deliberate gesture) is a quick way to bring
the voice back to its normal strength when a speaker notices it fading.

## outcome-focus
strategy: audience_focus
patterns: confidence_drop, sustained_stress, stress_creep
hint: Think message, not mechanics
action: Focus on what you want them to understand, not how you sound
pause: Pause. Say the one idea they must get, plainly
why: Watching yourself too closely under pressure makes skilled performance worse.
source: Beilock 2001

Research on "choking under pressure" shows that pressure leads people to monitor and control well-practised
skills step by step, and that this self-monitoring makes performance worse. Speaking is a well-practised skill.
Keeping attention on the goal (the listener understanding the idea) instead of on one's own voice and face lets it
run more smoothly.

## pace-full-stops
strategy: silent_pause
patterns: rushing, rushing_nervous
hint: Full stop = short breath
action: End each sentence, breathe, then start the next one
pause: Stop. Breathe once, then continue a little slower
why: Nerves remove the pauses first; putting them back slows the whole talk.
source: SUSO

Speakers who speed up rarely talk faster within words; they drop the pauses between sentences. Delivery chapters
recommend treating each full stop as a place to breathe and let the idea land. Putting the pauses back is easier
than trying to slow every word, and the overall rate falls on its own.

## freeze-gesture
strategy: gesture
patterns: freeze, filler_burst
hint: Let your hands find it
action: Gesture the idea; the words will follow
pause: Pause. Show the idea with your hands, then say it
why: Gesturing helps you retrieve the words you are looking for.
source: Krauss 1998

Gestures seem to help speakers find words: in studies, people who could not move their hands had more trouble
retrieving words and paused more. When you are stuck or filling with "um", moving the hands to show the idea
(its size, shape, direction or steps) often brings the word back.
