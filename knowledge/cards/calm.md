# Tip cards: calming the body (stress, tension, freeze)
# Format: "## id", then key: value lines, then a blank line and the passage (what retrieval searches
# and what the LLM is grounded on). hint = level 1 (glance), action = level 2, pause = level 3.

## breath-long-exhale
strategy: breathing
patterns: sustained_stress, masked_tension, rushing_nervous, confidence_drop, freeze
hint: Breathe out slowly
action: At your next full stop, breathe out slowly for 4 seconds
pause: Stop. One long breath out, look up, then go on
why: A long exhale is the quickest brake on a racing body.
source: Zaccaro 2018

Slow breathing, around six breaths per minute with an exhale longer than the inhale, is linked in many studies
to more parasympathetic ("rest") activity, higher heart rate variability and lower reported anxiety and arousal.
For a speaker this is useful because breathing is the one part of the stress response you can control while
talking: a full stop in your talk is a natural moment for one slow, long breath out.

## breath-sigh
strategy: breathing
patterns: sustained_stress, masked_tension, freeze
hint: Sigh it out
action: Between sentences: small double inhale, long sigh out
pause: Pause: two short breaths in, one long sigh out
why: Exhale-focused breathing lowered arousal fastest in a 2023 trial.
source: Balban 2023

In a month-long randomised study, a few minutes a day of "cyclic sighing" (a double inhale through the nose
followed by a long, slow exhale) improved mood and lowered breathing rate more than mindfulness meditation did.
The exhale is the active part. In a talk, one such sigh fits in a natural pause and is barely visible.

## tension-voice-release
strategy: relax_voice
patterns: masked_tension, sustained_stress
hint: Relax your throat
action: Drop your shoulders and let your voice sit lower
pause: Pause. Shoulders down, jaw loose, breathe, then restart lower
why: Tension in the neck and breath raises pitch and tightens the voice.
source: SUSO

Speaking anxiety shows up in the voice before it shows on the face: a higher pitch, a tight, breathy or shaky
sound and rushed phrases. Delivery chapters advise breathing from the abdomen and relaxing the shoulders, neck
and jaw so the voice can return to its normal pitch and resonance. The audience usually cannot see your nerves,
but they can hear a strained voice.

## ground-feet
strategy: ground_posture
patterns: sustained_stress, confidence_drop, masked_tension
hint: Feet flat, stand tall
action: Plant both feet and let your shoulders drop
pause: Pause. Feel both feet on the floor, then continue
why: A stable stance gives nervous energy somewhere to go.
source: EPS

Speaking anxiety often comes out as shifting weight, swaying or fidgeting, which in turn makes the speaker feel
less steady. Textbook advice is to stand with feet shoulder-width apart and weight even, which looks composed and
gives a physical anchor to return to when nerves rise.

## reframe-excited
strategy: reframe
patterns: sustained_stress, confidence_drop, masked_tension
hint: This is excitement
action: Tell yourself "I'm excited", not "calm down"
pause: Pause and think: "I'm excited to share this"
why: Calling the racing heart excitement improved speeches in a study.
source: Brooks 2014

Trying to calm down when the body is already highly aroused is hard. In experiments, people who said "I am
excited" before giving a speech were rated as more persuasive, competent and confident than people who said
"I am calm". Excitement and anxiety feel similar in the body; naming it excitement keeps the energy but points it
at an opportunity instead of a threat.

## audience-message
strategy: audience_focus
patterns: sustained_stress, confidence_drop, face_tense
hint: Think about the message
action: Focus on what your listener needs from this point
pause: Pause. Ask yourself: what is the one idea they must get?
why: Attention on yourself feeds anxiety; attention on the message lowers it.
source: Bodie 2010

Reviews of public speaking anxiety describe ruminative, self-focused thoughts ("I look nervous", "I'm going to
forget") as a core part of the problem. Approaches that move attention from the self to the audience and the
message, treating the talk as communication rather than a performance being judged, reduce that self-monitoring.

## smile-relax
strategy: smile_relax
patterns: face_tense, sustained_stress, confidence_drop
hint: Soften your face
action: Relax your jaw and give a small smile
pause: Pause. Unclench your jaw, smile at the camera, go on
why: Smiling during stress sped up heart-rate recovery in a lab study.
source: Kraft 2012

In a lab study, people who held a smile while doing stressful tasks had lower heart rate during recovery than
people with a neutral expression, even when the smile was not felt. A tense brow or clenched jaw also reads to
the audience as worry; a relaxed, open face reads as confidence.
